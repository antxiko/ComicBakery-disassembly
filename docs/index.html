What turned up when we took it apart

The carts were raccoons

The listing called carts what the baker shoots and what costs the baker a life. Drawn from the ROM they are the raccoons: two sprites in a row, the second sixteen pixels to the right, in grey, yellow and red (0x51B2).

Two bakeries

There is one bakery, the 92 columns at 0x7604, but the stage set-up picks its graphics blocks with (0xE051): green machines on stages 1, 2, 4, 6... and blue ones on 3, 5, 7... Checked against openMSX down to 0 bytes.

The sprites are not set up with the screen

The 64 sprite patterns are loaded by 0x70D6: three compressed blocks and, on top, their mirror images -each byte with its bits reversed and the two halves swapped-, so every figure faces both ways without being stored twice.

The demo plays from a script

While in the menu, the controller reading does not come from the controller: it comes from 80 bytes recorded at 0x6001, which the cartridge serves as if someone were pressing.

An eight-minute working day

The clock at 0x4614 counts seconds in BCD up to 60 and adds a minute; on minute eight it raises the end-of-stage flag. Neither of its two batch siblings has it.

The viewer left inside

0x60B0 fills the 768 cells with indices 0, 1, 2... to see every pattern at once and stays on a jr $. Nothing calls it: it is the developer's tool, left behind in the ROM.
